Dental care

Dental disease is the problem owners notice last and pets suffer from longest. Cleanings can start as early as one year of age.

Start from
One year of age
Applies to
Dogs and cats
Why it matters
Oral bacteria reach the bloodstream

Studies show that both dogs and cats need to begin regular cleanings as young as one year of age. It sounds early. The reason is that the damage begins long before anything is visible to an owner.

What happens without regular cleaning

Without regular cleaning and brushing, pets develop a thin layer of bacteria on their teeth and gum line. That layer hardens into tartar and plaque. Left unchecked, the bacteria cause tooth decay and gum disease, which makes it difficult for a dog or cat to eat most foods.

Why it is not only about teeth

Bacteria in the mouth can enter the bloodstream and cause damage to the heart, liver and kidneys, shortening the life expectancy of the pet. A mouth problem becomes an organ problem, quietly, over years.

Prevention costs less than treatment

Preventative care is always more effective and less costly than treatment of disease, and this is as true of your pet's dental health as of anything else we do here.

When a cleaning is needed

A dental cleaning is important if your pet has a large amount of tartar, has loose or decaying teeth, or did not lose all of their baby teeth. Tartar carries bacteria that can cause dental infection, infection on the heart valves, and kidney disease.

If you are not sure where your pet stands, mention it at their next visit and we will look.
