Puppies and kittens

The first year, in order

Puppies and kittens receive some immunity from their mother's milk, but that immunity only protects them for a short period of time. After it fades, they need a course of their own.

They require several booster vaccinations, given three weeks apart, to protect them from viruses common to this area — at six, nine, twelve and fifteen weeks of age. Annual vaccines are then recommended one year after the final puppy or kitten booster.

Missing a vaccination can lead to severe illness, which can lead to death, so the schedule is worth keeping to.

Good to know

Questions most new clients ask

How often should we come in?

At least once a year for a healthy pet — to boost vaccinations, catch problems showing no obvious signs, and monitor their aging process.

When to spay or neuter?

We recommend not sterilizing any earlier than six months of age, so your pet matures with their reproductive organs and hormones intact.

What about heartworms?

They are especially prevalent in south Mississippi. Both dogs and cats can contract them. If your pet is not on prevention, bring them in for an evaluation.

Do teeth really matter?

Studies show dogs and cats need regular cleanings from as young as one year. Oral bacteria can reach the heart, liver and kidneys.

Boarding with us

We recommend pets be current on vaccinations before a stay. If a booster is due or you are unsure, contact us four weeks in advance.

Which animals do you treat?

Dogs and cats. We began in 1975 as a mixed animal practice and became a small animal clinic exclusively in 2005.
