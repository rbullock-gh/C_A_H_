Columbia Animal Hospital offers a state-of-the-art surgical suite equipped with innovative tools to care for your pet. Surgery is the part of veterinary medicine owners worry about most, so it is worth explaining exactly how we approach it.

Blood work before anesthesia

Prior to surgery, our veterinarians recommend running blood work on your pet. Blood work can indicate issues that would not be found by a physical examination alone — and knowing about them beforehand is what allows anesthesia to be administered as safely as possible.

This is not an upsell. It is the difference between giving a drug to a patient whose liver and kidney function you know, and giving it to one whose you do not.

Monitoring, before and after

Before and after the procedure, the veterinarian constantly monitors your pet's vital signs. After treatment, your pet is kept warm while they continue to be monitored, recovering from anesthesia somewhere quiet rather than being sent home half-asleep.

When to have your pet spayed or neutered

Columbia Animal Hospital recommends not having your pet sterilized any earlier than six months of age. This allows your pet to mature with their reproductive organs and the corresponding hormones, which supports a normal aging process.

Arranging a procedure

Surgery is scheduled by phone. Call the hospital, tell us what your pet needs, and we will talk you through what happens on the day, what to bring, and when you can collect them.
