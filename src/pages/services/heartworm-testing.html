Columbia Animal Hospital places special emphasis on the prevention, testing and treatment of heartworm disease. If you live in or around Columbia, this is not a theoretical risk.

Why south Mississippi is different

Mosquitoes that have previously taken a blood meal from a heartworm-positive animal can pass immature heartworms to dogs and cats by biting them. Heartworms are especially prevalent in south Mississippi because of the high number of mosquitoes that carry them. The same warm, wet climate that makes the summers what they are makes heartworm a year-round conversation.

Testing

Accurate blood tests are available to determine whether a pet has heartworm disease, and the veterinarians at Columbia Animal Hospital can perform these tests — often having results while you wait. There is no sending a sample away and waiting days to hear.

Prevention

Medications are available to prevent both dogs and cats from becoming infected with heartworms, and Columbia Animal Hospital stocks these products at the clinic with a veterinarian's recommendation behind them.

If your pet is not currently on heartworm prevention, our doctors recommend bringing them in for an evaluation rather than simply starting a product.

Prevention is cheaper than treatment

Preventing heartworm disease is always less expensive and more effective than treating it once a pet has contracted these dangerous parasites. Treatment is hard on an animal in a way that a monthly preventative is not.
